Ludo Courtyard Privacy Policy

Last updated: 21 October 2026

Ludo Courtyard ("the app") is a dice board game. This policy explains what happens to your information when you use it.

Playing on your own phone

When you play against the computer or pass the phone around, nothing leaves your device. The app has no accounts, analytics, advertising or tracking.

Information stored on your device

The app keeps the names you type for players, your saved house rules, the game in progress, the dice ledger (how often each number was rolled for each player), wins, and your settings. This stays on your device. Uninstalling the app or clearing its data deletes it.

Playing online with friends

Online play is optional. When you make or join a room, the app sends the following through the Supabase Realtime service (servers in Mumbai, India) to the other people in the same room, while you play:

  • the room's six-letter code;
  • the name you type for online play (it does not need to be your real name);
  • a random tag the app makes for your phone, so the room can tell players apart (it is not linked to you or your device's identifiers);
  • the game itself: rolls and moves.

The app does not store any of this on a server; messages are passed on to the room and not kept. Anyone who knows a room's code can join that room, so share codes only with people you want to play with. Supabase, which runs the service, may keep standard technical logs (such as connection times and IP addresses) under its own privacy policy: supabase.com/privacy. The connection is encrypted.

Sharing a picture

If you tap "Share the story", the app makes a picture of the match and hands it to the app you choose. Nothing is sent anywhere else.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13.

Changes

If a future version adds features that handle data (such as ads), this policy will be updated before that version is released, and the date above will change.
